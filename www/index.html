Meat Thermometer 101

The numbers.

Check the thickest part with a food thermometer, away from bone or fat. Timers estimate cooking time; they cannot confirm food safety. Use the minimum temperatures below before serving.

USDA Minimum Safe Temps

Beef, Lamb (whole cuts) 145°F + 3 min rest
Pork 145°F + 3 min rest
Ground beef, pork & lamb 160°F
All poultry, including turkey burgers 165°F
Fish / Seafood 145°F

Steak doneness

These descriptions are for whole-cut steak preferences. Any temperature below 145°F is below USDA guidance. They do not apply to poultry or ground meat.

Rare

120–125°F (49–52°C)

Cool, bright red center. Silky texture. Color and touch cannot confirm a safe temperature.

Medium-Rare

130–135°F (54–57°C)

Warm, pink-red center. Slightly firmer, juicy. Below the USDA minimum for whole cuts.

Medium

140–145°F (60–63°C)

Pink throughout, brown outer band. Firm but still moist. For whole cuts, reach at least 145°F and rest 3 minutes.

Well Done

160°F+ (71°C+)

No pink. Fully browned throughout. Firmer texture. Confirm temperature with a thermometer.

At the grill

  • Insert probe into the thickest part, away from bone or fat
  • Carryover heat varies by cut and thickness. Confirm poultry reaches 165°F and ground beef, pork or lamb reaches 160°F before removing from heat
  • Whole cuts of beef, pork and lamb need 145°F followed by at least 3 minutes of rest
  • Pat dry before grilling — steam is the enemy of a good sear
  • Keep meat refrigerated until preparation. Avoid leaving it out for more than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F
  • Don't press the meat — you're squeezing out the juices
